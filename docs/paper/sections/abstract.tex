\begin{abstract}
Fraud rings share devices, cards and network addresses and move money among
themselves, which graph queries can expose. Two obstacles appear on live
transaction streams. Legitimate shared infrastructure such as office gateways,
carrier address translation and kiosks links many unrelated accounts, and the
usual remedy, a hard degree cap, hides any ring that shares a busy attribute.
Whole-graph queries re-run on a timer also cost more as the graph grows. We
replace the cap with a continuous hub-aware weight with time decay, detect
incrementally on the neighbourhood touched by each micro-batch, and pass
candidate rings through a calibrated scorer that explains every alert. On a
deterministic simulator with ring-level ground truth and five evasion
strategies, over \EScenarios{} scenarios and \ESeeds{} test seeds, the method
reaches a ring F1 score of \EPropF{} against \ECapTenF{} for the original
degree-capped Cypher rules and \ELouvainF{} for Louvain communities, and every
difference is significant in a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Hub weighting
extends the reachable recall on shared-attribute rings from \FrCapSMaxR{} to
\FrHubSMaxR{}, though not at higher precision everywhere, and the scorer
contributes most of the precision. Incremental detection flags the same rings
as full recomputation and, with \ScaleMaxUsers{} users, takes \ScaleIncMs{} ms
per batch against \ScaleFullMs{} ms. Rings that rotate identities or act slowly
evade every method we tested. All positive results are synthetic, and we state
what the public anti-money laundering datasets we could use can and cannot test.
\end{abstract}
