None of the public datasets we could obtain has device, card or IP links, so on
all of them only the transaction detectors (temporal cycle, smurfing) run, and
the results below say nothing about hub-aware weighting
(Table~\ref{tab:public}; sources and hashes in \texttt{docs/datasets.md}).

\textbf{AMLworld} \cite{altman2023amlworld} (HI-Small) is the only one with
group labels: 370 laundering attempts of eight typologies. We replayed the first
four days (\PubAmlRows{} transactions, \PubAmlAccounts{} accounts) with a
\PubAmlWindow{}\,h window and used the \PubAmlGroups{} attempts that lie
entirely in that slice as ring ground truth. The detectors confirmed
\PubAmlGroupsFlagged{} groups; attempt recall is \PubAmlGroupR{} and
account-level precision and recall are \PubAmlAccP{} and \PubAmlAccR{}.
In other words, the transaction rules matched none of the attempts, and the
groups they did confirm were almost all benign. Attempts span a median of
75\,h, far longer than the window. A one-day window was too slow on our test
machine (throughput fell steadily as the window filled), so attempts whose
transfers are hours apart cannot be matched; and AMLworld's typologies (fan-in,
scatter-gather, bipartite, stacks) are mostly not the relayed cycles and
structuring our two transaction rules look for. The result is a negative one
for short-window transaction rules on this data, not a comparison with
published AMLworld models, which classify individual transactions.

\textbf{Elliptic}, as used in \cite{badjie2026ensembles,kumar2026tagat}, links each Bitcoin transaction to
the later transactions spending its outputs, so its graph has no cycles, and
amounts are not published. Neither transaction rule can fire: no account was
flagged (\PubEllFlagged{} of \PubEllAccounts{}). We report it as not applicable
to group detection rather than as a score.

\textbf{AMLSim} samples (IBM, 20{,}000 accounts, 200 planted cycles and/or
fan-ins) were not flagged either (\PubSimCycleFlagged{}, \PubSimFaninFlagged{}
and \PubSimBothFlagged{} accounts). The planted cycles are not time-ordered:
none of the 21 we could reconstruct from equal hop amounts has its hops in time
order (one cycle's five hops fall at steps 28, 113, 84, 59 and 78), whereas our
cycle detector, by design, looks for money relayed around a loop.
